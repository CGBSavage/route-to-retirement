About this tool

A member of the postal family built this to help with two big decisions: when to retire and when to start drawing Social Security — for a conservative, fairly straightforward financial situation. It doesn't include every factor that might be part of your big picture, but it does offer a rich set of tools, including what a working spouse needs: their own tax-deferred savings, and their own retirement and Social Security decisions to make.

We've made it public so our small group of postal friends and family can plug in their numbers and think through their own retirement planning. I am not a financial planner. The decision of when to start Social Security goes well beyond "wait longer and the check is bigger" — many things need to be weighed together, and my hope is that this grows into a genuinely useful place to start: a way to make your own finances help you plan a realistic, doable retirement, one where you might not have to scrimp as much as you feared.

It's amazing what a difference even trimming a few hundred dollars a month off your spending now makes to your long-term plans. Try it and see.

    Privacy & terms

    What's stored, and where. To save you retyping, the numbers you enter are kept in this browser's own storage on this device — not on any server, and never sent anywhere. Nothing you type includes your name, Social Security number, or account numbers. That storage isn't encrypted: anyone who uses this same browser on this device could open the page and see your entries, the same as any website that "remembers" you. On a shared or borrowed device, turn saving off or clear your entries when you're done.

    Terms, in plain English. Route2Retirement is a free educational tool made by and for postal workers. It gives rough estimates from the numbers you enter and standard assumptions; it is not financial, tax, or legal advice, and it can be wrong. Nothing here is a promise about what you'll receive from FERS, TSP, or Social Security — confirm those with HR/OPM, tsp.gov, and ssa.gov, and talk to a qualified planner before deciding. It's provided as-is, without warranty; you use it at your own risk. Not affiliated with, or endorsed by, the U.S. Postal Service, OPM, or the Social Security Administration.

    Please read: This is an educational estimate, not financial, tax, or investment advice, and it can't see your whole picture (health insurance, taxes, FEHB, Medicare, sick-leave credit, market swings). Before you file anything, confirm your pension numbers with an official annuity estimate from HR/OPM, your Social Security numbers at ssa.gov, and talk it over with a fee-only financial planner if you can.
